Abstract

In this work, we study whether frontier models can continually improve real-world AI infrastructure over long horizons. Existing evaluations often focus on individual components or short optimization budgets, limiting our understanding of sustained optimization across the infrastructure stack. To address this gap, we introduce Infrathon, a benchmark of diverse, long-horizon AI infrastructure optimization tasks. We evaluate six models across 150 runs with 12- or 24-hour budgets, totaling over 10,000 GPU-hours. Our results show that model rankings vary with the evaluation horizon, while 135 of the 150 runs achieve further improvements during the second half of their budgets. These findings indicate that shorter evaluations can overlook both optimization gains and changes in relative model performance. We also find agents can discover solutions that outperform human-designed baselines: Claude Opus 5.5 increases the mean KV-cache compression ratio from 6.82× with the human-designed Quant-VideoGen codec to 19.86×. Analysis of agent trajectories identifies task violations, unreliable testing, and premature completion as barriers to sustained improvement. Together, our Infrathon framework provides resources for constructing long-horizon tasks, evaluating frontier agents, and discovering good infrastructure solutions.

Scoring and token definitions

Rank the six models by each task’s best valid within-budget score, then average 100 × (6 − rank) / 5 equally across tasks. Exact ties share average rank; a run without a valid scored submission receives zero. The 95% task-bootstrap confidence intervals use 10,000 resamples for the full suite and 3,000 for filtered groups.

Visible tokens use o200k_base for agent text and tool-call content, excluding tool responses, repeated code-diff displays, and reasoning traces. Full-suite totals aggregate all 25 tasks; filtered totals sum rounded per-task values (≈). First-score time is the median across scored runs in the selected tasks.
